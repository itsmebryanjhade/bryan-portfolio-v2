I started learning to code in Grade 11 while taking HUMSS. I had intended to take STEM, so my strand didn't match the direction I wanted to pursue. But I was interested in software, and I wanted to keep exploring it.

That interest started with two of my uncles. Both work in tech, and they influenced me to try coding for myself. When I did, I found it fun. I liked seeing how software worked and how people interacted with it. I loved being able to build something.

During my free time, I watched coding videos on YouTube. When school projects or activities gave me a chance to include something related to tech, I took it. I was still doing my work as a HUMSS student while finding opportunities to practice what interested me.

## Finding things to build at school

After finishing Grade 11, I built OdizeeBooth, the project now called Kisap.

Our school was using third party photobooth software. I wanted to create something the school could control and use for free. It gave me a practical reason to build: there was already a use for it.

Before Grade 12 classes started, a classmate brought me into the Odizee Student Council, or OSC, as an officer. I proposed OdizeeBooth, and we used it during Balik Eskwela. It went well, and something I had built became part of an actual school event.

My work in the council also included photography and documentation. I took photos during events and meetings, which meant having plenty of images to prepare afterward.

Adding frames with our logos, social accounts, and other details could take hours when there were many photos. That repetitive work gave me another idea.

I built Framori, a tool that could apply frames to photos in batches. We used it for our photos, giving me another way to connect coding with the work I was already doing for the council.

## Building Halalan alongside schoolwork

Before I graduated, the council needed to hold an election for its next officers. The existing process used Google Forms, followed by tallying the votes.

Our school had a computer lab, so I saw an opportunity to build a voting system we could use there. That became Halalan 2026.

One of the hardest parts was compiling the names of more than 800 students into the system. Getting all those names together was a substantial part of the work.

I was also building Halalan alongside my schoolwork. The system needed my time, but I still had responsibilities as a student. Both were happening at once, which made the project challenging to finish.

More than 800 students eventually voted using Halalan. We completed the election without system errors or crashes.

By the time I graduated, coding had become part of how I contributed to my school. It had taken me from watching tutorials in my free time to building tools for events, photography, and an election.

## What I would tell another student

I still want to work toward becoming a senior software developer. Taking HUMSS didn't remove that goal or stop me from beginning.

I also believe a different strand can still contribute something useful to your journey. You don't have to dismiss what you're studying to keep pursuing another interest.

If you're a student who feels you chose the wrong strand, don't let that feeling become the reason you stop trying. If coding interests you, give yourself opportunities to learn it. Use your free time when you can, and look for ways to apply it to the things around you.

Your strand is part of your education. You can keep learning beyond it and continue toward the work you want to do.
