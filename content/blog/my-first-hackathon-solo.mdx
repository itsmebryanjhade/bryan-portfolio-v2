I joined my first hackathon as a first year student because I wanted experience. I wanted to understand how the event worked, learn the mechanics, and have a better idea of what to expect the next time I joined one.

My submission was PondoPath, and I built it myself. Most of the other competitors had teams. I went in on my own, which is something I give myself credit for. Joining alone took courage, even though I was mainly there to learn.

## Presenting felt like a different level

The part I didn't expect was the pressure of presenting. I had to stand in front of many people, including business owners and fellow competitors, then go through five minutes of Q&A.

For a first experience, it felt like a high level to step into immediately. I had built my submission, and now I had to explain it in front of that audience.

One question stayed with me. I was asked what I would do if someone copied a specific feature from my app and implemented it in their own product.

I became so nervous that I blanked, even though I had an answer in mind. I couldn't get that answer out at the time. That is the moment I keep coming back to when I think about the experience.

## What I wanted to say

The answer I wanted to give was that I wouldn't react simply because someone copied a feature. In my view, copying features is difficult to avoid. I would focus on maintaining the quality of my own features and giving users a reason to keep using my product.

I had that answer in mind, but my nerves kept me from saying it during the Q&A. Being able to think through a question and explain my response under pressure were two things I needed to prepare for together.

## What I would do differently

For my next hackathon, I want to give more attention to presenting and demonstrating the app. I came away from this event feeling how much the actual, visible product mattered to the people watching.

I also want to know my own work well enough to explain it clearly when questions come. Building the submission is part of the preparation, and so is helping an audience understand what I made.

The other thing I want to work on is staying calm and allowing myself to enjoy the experience. My nervousness got in the way of what I wanted to communicate. I want to handle that part better when I join again.

Whether you go alone or with a team, my advice from this first experience is to prepare for the presentation and demo, understand what you built, and try to enjoy being there.
