Taking photos was only part of my documentation work for the Odizee Student Council. After school events and meetings, I still had to prepare the images and add our event frames, including the logos, social accounts, and other details.

A typical batch had around 50 to 80 photos, sometimes more. I added the frame to each photo manually, one at a time. The design could be the same across the batch, but I still had to repeat the work for every image.

That was the task I wanted Framori to handle.

## Framing the whole batch

The flow starts with the frame design. I drop in a frame with a transparent background, leaving only the artwork that should appear around the photo. Framori identifies whether it is portrait or landscape, and I can provide both orientations.

Then I drop in all the photos together. It generates the framed images and puts them into a ZIP. I download it, extract the files, and the batch is ready.

For my own batches of around 50 to 80 or more photos, that process took about five minutes. I could prepare the frames and process the photos together instead of applying the design to each image individually.

## Getting the photo quality right

The hardest development problem was the quality of the finished photos.

That became a bottleneck because I couldn't find what was causing the issue. Producing a batch quickly would only be useful if the finished images were good enough to use for our documentation. I needed to get that part right as well as the framing process.

I eventually resolved the problem with AI's help during development. It helped me get past an issue I had struggled to identify, and I was able to continue with the tool.

## A tool for work I already had

Framori came from a task I was doing regularly. I already had the photos and the frame designs. What I needed was a way to bring them together across a whole batch without repeating the same manual steps.

That made the result easy for me to judge: could I get the framed photos I needed, at a usable quality, with less work?

The change was concrete. A batch that meant opening and framing dozens of photos individually became a process of adding the frames, dropping in the photos, and extracting the finished ZIP. Framori handled the repetition so I could finish the batch and move on.
